# 10. HOLLOW SAINT

> Seed: `titan` · Goal fit **31/40** · Distinctiveness **4/5** · Feasibility **2/5** · Verdict: **promising**

**Pitch.** A real-time survival-automation game set inside the continent-sized body of a dying god. You and your colony are parasites-turned-surgeons who tap its arteries for power, graft its organs into living machinery, and race to build a way out before it dies and takes you with it — all while its immune system treats every incision as a wound to be swarmed, walled off, and burned. Every drop of the titan's lifeblood you spend on your factory is a drop not keeping you, or it, alive.

**The hook.** It's a survival-automation game where your entire factory is a living organism you grow inside a dying god, and building beautifully is literally how you hide from its immune system.

**Tone.** Reverent body-horror crossed with tender stewardship: you are desecrating and nursing something vast and sacred at the same time. Art is top-down/isometric hand-drawn 2D — a "wet cathedral" of translucent membranes, bone-white struts, and rivers of glowing amber ichor — where aliveness comes cheaply from shader tricks (scrolling flow along vessels, a slow global pulse, palette shifts from warm amber = alive to grey-green necrosis to angry inflamed red) layered over organic tiles, well within a small team's reach for the browser.

## How it serves the founding goals

### Goal 1 — constant pressure

Three coupled pressures that never resolve, only trade off. (1) VITALITY — a global stat that is always ticking down because the titan is dying; it sets ambient warmth, artery pressure (your free power), and how hard the immune system can fight. It is your story clock and it never stops. (2) ANTIGEN — a biological pollution analog. Every incision, graft, and running machine emits antigen that pools locally and globally and summons the immune response. (3) The IMMUNE SYSTEM ITSELF, which is simultaneously enemy and weather: leukocyte patrols swarm high-antigen tiles; macrophage hunters "tag" and home in on players who camp; and inflammation is literal weather — high antigen heats a region, floods it with caustic ichor, and triggers cytokine-storm hazards. It also reshapes terrain via fibrosis, growing scar walls that encase and crush your buildings. The cruel inversion keeps it interesting instead of grindy: a HEALTHIER titan has a STRONGER immune system but a livable environment (power, warmth); a DYING titan has a feeble immune system but a lethal environment (necrosis spreads, arteries lose pressure so your power dies, and it thrashes in feverish spasms). There is no safe equilibrium — you are always sliding between "the body is fighting me" and "the body is rotting under me," and the sweet spot drifts as you harvest. Pressure stays fresh because the SAME dial (Vitality) is your resource richness, your power supply, your enemy's strength, and your deadline, so reading it is a live strategic act, not a chore bar to top off.

### Goal 2 — the automation layers

The metaphor is surgery + cultivation: you do not bolt together dead machines, you graft living organs that must be perfused with ichor, that age and necrotize, and that emit antigen while they run. Two logistics networks are coupled — the PRODUCTION line and a parallel PERFUSION (circulatory) network that keeps the production line alive. Layers: (0) MANUAL — you harvest tissue with a lancet, carry organs in your gut-pack, hand-crank a press for ichor, hand-feed grafts. (1) PERISTALTIC VESSELS — grafted vein/gut tubing that pumps fluids and chunked solids by peristalsis; the "belt," but it needs ichor pressure, and it clots or ruptures under strain. Alleviates hauling. (2) SPHINCTER & GANGLION LOGIC — valves and nerve-clusters (built from neural filament) that route and gate flow on conditions; the "circuit network." Alleviates you standing there directing traffic. (3) DIGESTIVE VATS & ENZYME GLANDS — grafted stomachs/glands that refine raw tissue, dissolve chitin, and brew enzymes. Alleviates hand-refining. (4) MARROW BEDS / CULTIVATORS — stem-culture farms that GROW myofiber, bone, and components in place instead of you going out to harvest. Alleviates expeditions. (5) MUSCLE ACTUATORS / CILIA FIELDS — grafted muscle that does physical work: cranks, doors, turrets. Alleviates manual grinding. (6) GANGLION CORTEX — a built "brain" that runs colony-wide policies: auto-balancing perfusion, dispatching defense, throttling antigen. Alleviates micromanagement. And a distinct stealth axis: (7) MASKING GLANDS that spend ichor to suppress your antigen signature. It is fresh vs. Factorio's inert belts because your factory has a METABOLISM and a HEAT SIGNATURE — you are not only optimizing throughput, you are running a body that gets hungry, gets old, and gets noticed.

### Goal 2 — the sacrifice economy

Ichor is the single currency of all four things you care about: powering/perfusing machines (automation), metabolizing to keep yourself warm and alive (survival), returning to arteries to raise Vitality (keeping the titan alive), and running masking glands (safety). So every liter poured into building is a liter not spent surviving — the sacrifice is baked into one shared resource. On top of that, three sharper sacrifices: TERRITORY (building anywhere raises local antigen, which invites swarms and fibrosis that can wall your base in — expansion literally paints a target), SAFETY (bigger automation is louder, so the payoff tier is also the most-hunted), and the big irreversible one — ORGAN HARVEST. Cracking a major organ is a one-time windfall AND permanently unlocks a new buildable line, but it permanently lowers max Vitality and triggers a titan-wide immune response. That is the seed's strip-mine-vs-sustain axis made mechanical. WORKED EXAMPLE, MINUTE 30: you have tapped an arteriole and hand-gathered myofiber; local antigen is climbing and a leukocyte patrol is inbound; a necrotic patch is creeping toward your tap two tiles away. You hold exactly enough ichor for ONE of: (a) a masking membrane to go quiet and keep harvesting by hand, (b) your first peristaltic vessel to stop hauling — but building it spikes antigen and the patrol will find you, or (c) cauterizing the necrosis before it eats your only power tap. Slow-and-hidden, faster-but-hunted, or defend-the-ground — and you can afford one. The invest-vs-survive choice hurts well because the thing that would end your busywork is the thing that most endangers you while it pays off.

### Goal 4 — the map changes the plan

The map is procedural ANATOMY, and it varies: (a) which major organs exist and where (some titans have three hearts, some vast lungs, some a bloated gut, some rich neural tissue) — this sets which power sources and resources are abundant; (b) vascular topology — where the great arteries run is where free ichor pressure (and thus good base sites) is; (c) pre-existing necrosis and infestations already eating parts at start; (d) cavity structure — open chambers vs. tight capillary mazes, setting defensibility and mobility; (e) titan "species" — a flooded Leviathan, a bone-dense Colossus, or a Chrysalis that metamorphoses over the run. Concrete reactions: (1) "Huge healthy heart but almost no neural tissue, so I had cheap ichor power but couldn't build smart ganglion logic — I went wide with dumb brute-force peristaltic routing and hand-directed flow instead of circuits." (2) "A necrotic gut spawned adjacent to my only safe cavity, so my first three hours were containment — cauterize and wall the rot — not growth." (3) "Three hearts meant I could sacrifice one whole heart for a windfall and still hold Vitality up, so aggressive strip-mining was viable here in a way it never is on a single-heart titan." The environment isn't set dressing; it decides whether you can even afford your preferred strategy.

### Goal 5 — base ⇄ combat loop

Combat is mostly against the immune system (leukocyte swarms, macrophage hunters, antibody clouds, and organ-GUARDIANS that gate the deep organs) plus rival infestations — other parasites nesting in the titan. BASE → COMBAT: (1) muscle-actuator turrets (harpoon-glands, acid-spitters) fire only when fed by your perfusion network, so defense throughput is tied to your ichor plumbing; (2) a harvested electric sub-organ, once grafted, powers an arc-fence and a stun lance; (3) a vaccination vat processes defeated leukocytes into cultured antigens you graft as armor, turning yesterday's attacker into today's resistance. COMBAT/EXPLORATION → BASE: (1) beating the LUNG guardian lets you safely harvest the lung and unlocks pneumatic "bellows" power — an entire second power source independent of artery pressure, huge when Vitality is low; (2) killing an internal predator and dissecting its venom gland unlocks caustic enzymes that upgrade your vats' refining; (3) reaching the neural tissue near the "eye" yields filament caches that unlock higher-tier ganglion logic (better automation). WORKED EXAMPLE, HOUR 20: the colony needs a mass myofiber supply to grow the Chrysalis escape-rib. The fastest source is the titan's secondary heart, guarded by a calcified macrophage-boss. Cracking it yields a massive ichor+tissue windfall AND unlocks the arc-organ weapon graft — but drops max Vitality ~20%, speeds the death clock, and triggers a server-wide fever (hostile weather for an hour). Your sustain-focused teammate is mid-heal on a lung to buy the colony time; cracking the heart partly undoes it. Strip now for a fast, brutal endgame, or grind cultivators longer and gamble the titan doesn't die of natural causes first.

### Goals 3 + 6 — many paths, no milestones

There is no tech tree and no research. Capabilities are HARVESTED: dissecting or processing any tissue type yields a GRAFT SCHEMA (knowledge encoded biologically), and you SPLICE schemas together to make hybrids (muscle-actuator + venom-gland = acid-piston; masking-gland + peristaltic-vessel = a "quiet" pipeline). Because which organs exist is map-dependent and which you harvest is your choice, your capability set is emergent and combinatorial rather than a fixed ladder — closer to ingredient/spellcrafting than to tiers. Nothing gates on "tier 3 unlocks tier 4"; progression is continuous because you are always incrementally improving perfusion efficiency, antigen suppression, and throughput, with no milestone banners. Three genuinely different viable archetypes players discover: (1) THE SYMBIOTE — keep Vitality high, footprint tiny, antigen near zero, grow everything from marrow beds; slow, safe, sustainable, thrives on the strong-immune/good-environment side of the dial and wins by masking. (2) THE BUTCHER — crack organs fast for windfalls, race capabilities, accept collapsing Vitality and a necrotic world; effectively a speedrun that must out-build the death clock and manage fever, not the immune system. (3) THE FIBROSIS-FARMER — deliberately provoke the immune system and HARVEST the response (culturing leukocytes and scar tissue as renewable feedstock), building in the most contested zones on purpose; combat-forward, turning the titan's defenses into a supply chain.

### Goal 7 — beauty pays rent

Native mechanic: the titan's body reads your construction as either SELF or FOREIGN, and treats foreign-looking sprawl as a tumor. It is computed as a "Belonging" score from the geometry and topology of what you build, feeding directly into local antigen (the survival system), so beauty is camouflage, not a bolted-on meter. Three inputs: (1) LAMINAR FLOW — vessels that curve smoothly and avoid sharp bends run quietly; each hard turn adds turbulence points that sum into antigen, so tangled plumbing literally irritates the body while flowing layouts stay hidden. (2) SYMMETRY & FRACTAL BRANCHING — building with bilateral/radial symmetry and proper calibre-tapering branches (thick trunk → arterioles → capillaries) grants a "harmony" multiplier that both perfuses more efficiently and lowers antigen; uniform fat pipes and blobby clumps do not. (3) INTEGRATION — aligning grafts to the grain of surrounding muscle and respecting an organ's form yields bonuses. The result is that the survival-optimal base is one that looks like it belongs inside a living creature: organic, flowing, symmetric, fractal — lovely because it is functional and hidden, and satisfying to build because you are quite literally playing surgeon making elegant anatomy. The immune system leaving you alone is the game telling you your work is beautiful.

### Goal 8 — why you're doing this

Outer frame: the titans are the last habitable places in a dead cosmos, and your colony fled into this one — but it is dying, and when Vitality hits zero the corpse goes cold and the Rot consumes everything, colony included. The single outer goal is DON'T DIE WITH THE TITAN, resolvable two ways the players choose between: build a Chrysalis seed-vessel and MIGRATE to the next titan, or heal the titan enough to PILOT it as a living ship. It is not a quest chain; it cascades through four mechanisms so you always know why you're doing the current thing. (1) The Vitality decline IS the story clock — watching it is watching the ending approach, and every action nudges it. (2) To build the Chrysalis / healing apparatus you need vast resources and capabilities, which requires automation, which requires antigen management — that's your minute-to-minute surgery and layout. (3) The capabilities to escape or heal are locked in the deep organs (heart, brain) behind guardians, so you must fight inward. (4) The strip-vs-sustain choice is the narrative's thesis made mechanical: strip-mine to escape faster but hasten the death you're fleeing, or sustain to buy time while the body fights you — the whole game is "how do you relate to the dying thing you live inside." The frame is delivered environmentally — the encysted remains of earlier colonies who failed, and the titan's dimly dreaming mind — never via a mission log.

## Multiplayer (2–20, persistent)

2-20 players share one persistent titan, one Vitality economy, and one interconnected base, which makes specialization emerge naturally: SURGEONS push deep and fight guardians for organs, VASCULAR ENGINEERS build and maintain the perfusion/logistics networks, IMMUNOLOGISTS run defense, decoys, and masking, and CULTIVATORS tend the marrow beds at home. Because Vitality is shared, the strip-vs-sustain axis becomes genuine social drama — a Butcher and a Symbiote on the same server are in real, productive tension. OFFLINE: the world persists and the titan keeps dying slowly; your running grafts keep eating shared ichor while you're gone, so logging off forces a choice — leave holdings RUNNING (productive but draining the colony and, if perfusion fails, necrotizing into an antigen mess for teammates) or put them in TORPOR/encyst them (they stop eating and stop working, decaying slowly and safely). Antigen generation is driven mostly by active play, so an empty server "rests" and calms, while a full one runs hot. PRESSURE SCALES WITH HEADCOUNT automatically: more players means more antigen and bigger immune waves, and more mouths draining Vitality faster — so a crowded titan is a hotter, shorter, more desperate run that demands the division of labor the setting already invites.

## Closest existing games

- Factorio — same automation DNA, but the machines are alive and hungry, pollution is a biological immune response, and there's no fixed tech tree
- Oxygen Not Included — shared systems/metabolism-sim and cascading failures, but real-time multiplayer with combat and a reactive enemy that is also the weather
- Rain World — the same alive, hostile, creature-as-world tone, but with deep base-building and factory-scale automation on top
- Subnautica — 'the alien body is the threat' exploration, but top-down 2D, co-op, and industrial-scale grafting
- Don't Starve Together — constant survival pressure, co-op, and stylized 2D build-or-die, but with layered automation and a single continent-sized organism as the map

## Designer-admitted risks

- Cognitive overload: two coupled logistics networks (production + perfusion) plus antigen and Vitality management may cross from 'rich' into 'exhausting.' Keeping grafts alive is exactly the upkeep-chore trap Factorio avoids by making buildings permanent; torpor, forgiving decay curves, and the ganglion cortex must do heavy lifting or the fantasy becomes janitorial.
- The aesthetics-as-camouflage mechanic is hard to make legible and fair. Algorithmically scoring symmetry/laminar-flow/branching and clearly telling the player WHY the body is angry is a serious UX and graphics problem; get it wrong and it reads as an arbitrary hidden beauty tax, or it converges everyone onto one 'optimal pretty' shape and kills expression.
- The strip-vs-sustain axis can collapse. If strip-mining reliably wins (or reliably loses), the game's central strategic dial dies; and the Vitality 'sweet spot' risks degenerating into babysitting a slider rather than making interesting choices. Requires very careful tuning across map types and player counts.
- No tech tree risks losing reward cadence and direction. Continuous emergent progression can leave players unmoored without the dopamine of visible milestone unlocks; graft-splicing needs strong in-world signposting or players stall and quit — a direct, ongoing tension with the 'no clear lines' goal that must be actively managed, not just wished away.
- Browser + real-time performance is genuinely ambitious. Simulating ichor pressure, antigen diffusion, and living-tissue metabolism across a continent-scale map for up to 20 concurrent players in a browser on fly.io is heavy; fluid/diffusion sims are expensive, and the abstractions needed to make it cheap may undercut the very aliveness that sells the game.

## Adversarial critique (greenlight review)

| Goal | Score | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Constant pressure | 4/5 | The Vitality inversion genuinely forbids a safe equilibrium, but the pitch ships its own pressure-release valves — masking glands and the near-zero-antigen Symbiote path let a careful player buy the pressure down to a slow global clock, and slow global clocks do not keep anyone moving minute to minute. |
| 2. Sacrifice-gated automation | 4/5 | Ichor-as-universal-currency makes invest-vs-survive native rather than bolted on, but living grafts that age, clot, emit antigen, and drain ichor forever mean automation re-denominates labor into upkeep instead of alleviating it — the 'investment pays off' half of this goal is asserted, not mechanically secured. |
| 3. Many solutions | 3/5 | The three archetypes are plausible economy-level strategies, but base-building convergence is baked in: the Belonging mechanic decrees one optimal shape (symmetric, laminar, fractal-tapered) on every map, and a single-currency economy tends to collapse every decision into 'maximize ichor.' |
| 4. Map reactivity | 4/5 | Organs as discrete, legible strategic anchors (no neural tissue = no logic tier; three hearts = strip-mining viable) genuinely generates run-level strategy beyond generic biome shuffling, though organ absence deletes pillars rather than reshaping them, which reads as a content-removal roll on bad seeds. |
| 5. Base ⇄ combat loop | 4/5 | Perfusion-fed turrets and dissect-to-graft make the loop structural in both directions, but chaining immune strength to Vitality means combat pressure deflates exactly as a run progresses — the base-to-combat half sags late-game when the titan is dying and the only 'enemy' left is weather. |
| 6. No milestones | 3/5 | 'No tech tree' is oversold: the seven numbered automation layers are a fixed chore-relief ladder every colony will climb in near-identical order, and organ guardians plus permanent organ-crack unlocks are milestone gates wearing a wet coat. |
| 7. Mechanical aesthetics | 4/5 | Immune self/foreign recognition is the best in-fiction justification for a beauty mechanic anyone has proposed, but scoring a single rubric (symmetry, curves, tapering) mandates a house style rather than rewarding beauty, and curved laminar plumbing fights top-down tile-grid construction at the engine level. |
| 8. Goal-driven | 5/5 | Vitality is simultaneously clock, power supply, enemy strength, and ending, so the narrative-to-action cascade is airtight — 'why am I doing this' is answered by reading one number; the only crack is late joiners inheriting a doomed persistent server they never chose. |

**Biggest weakness.** The living-factory metabolism inverts the genre's core payoff: every automation layer adds perfusion demand, aging, clotting, antigen emission, and offline ichor drain, so investment compounds maintenance surface instead of leverage — the exact opposite of the Factorio dopamine the sacrifice loop is supposed to buy. The pitch's own mitigations (torpor, forgiving decay curves, ganglion cortex) are tuning hopes, not designs, and the problem collides head-on with the shared-ichor multiplayer economy, where your sleeping automation taxes your teammates.

**How a real server dies (failure scenario).** Hour 10, four friends on a persistent titan. Two log off and choose 'leave running' because torpor would stall the myofiber line; overnight their grafts drain the shared ichor pool, perfusion pressure drops below a clot threshold in one vessel, an entire wing necrotizes, and the antigen bloom summons a macrophage wave. The two players online that evening spend three straight hours cauterizing dead friends' plumbing and rebuilding at low artery pressure — pure janitorial triage, zero building — while the Belonging system silently penalizes their rushed right-angle emergency vessels, spiking antigen further for reasons the UI cannot explain. The offline pair return to encysted rubble and a Vitality clock 15% lower, conclude the factory is a pet that punishes ownership, and the server dies not to the immune system but to a group chat that goes quiet.

**The steelman fix.** Add earned permanence: a graft that sustains high Belonging and healthy perfusion long enough is ACCEPTED as self-tissue — it permanently stops decaying, stops emitting antigen, and stops drawing shared ichor while idle, whereas sloppy builds stay parasitic and rot. This one rule makes automation finally net out labor (rescuing goal 2), gives the beauty mechanic the strongest possible reward — permanence itself — instead of a hidden tax (goal 7), and eliminates the offline-drain griefing that will kill co-op servers, all while keeping the sacrifice at build time where it belongs.

**Distinctiveness (4/5).** Closest game: Factorio. The pollution-evolution-biter economy is structurally Factorio's skeleton and no amount of viscera hides it, but no shipped title combines automation with an immune-system antagonist, a metabolizing map, and beauty-as-camouflage — the setting differs enough to matter if, and only if, the upkeep treadmill is solved.

**Feasibility (2/5).** The server simulation is the killer: a continuously solved perfusion flow network coupled to antigen diffusion fields, immune agent swarms, dynamic fibrosis terrain deformation, and a geometry-analyzing Belonging scorer, all persistent and real-time for up to 20 browser clients on fly.io-class hardware. Each subsystem is individually shippable; together they are a multi-year systems project, and the graph-abstraction shortcuts required will drain the very aliveness the pitch sells. Hand-drawn organic art with curved vessel splines and deforming terrain is also a far larger art/tech lift than tile-based machinery sprites.
