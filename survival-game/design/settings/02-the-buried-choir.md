# 2. THE BURIED CHOIR — Desert of a Shattered God

> Seed: `godsand` · Goal fit **35/40** · Distinctiveness **4/5** · Feasibility **2/5** · Verdict: **promising**

**Pitch.** A real-time co-op survival-automation game set on the corpse of a fallen god, where every machine is a resurrected relic — golem, obelisk, clockwork shrine — that drinks from the same waterskin you do. The desert is alive: sandstorms rebury your works and exhume new ruins, and your base's power grid is a literal song whose harmony depends on how beautifully you build. Reassemble the god organ by organ and decide, together, what it becomes.

**The hook.** A factory game where every machine is a resurrected fragment of a dead god that drinks from the same waterskin you do — and your base's power grid is a song that sounds exactly as good as it's built, on a map the wind keeps burying and exhuming.

**Tone.** Bronze-age mythpunk rendered in flat-shaded isometric 2D: ochre dunes, verdigris copper, bone-white ruins, long hard shadows that matter mechanically (shade is a resource), with rare shocks of turquoise water and gold leaf. Emotionally it is reverent and lonely-then-communal — you are tiny scavengers singing a dead giant back to life, and the audio direction (your base is audibly a choir) does half the atmospheric work, which is cheap to ship in a browser compared to art.

## How it serves the founding goals

### Goal 1 — constant pressure

Three stacked pressures, none of which is a flat decay timer. (1) THIRST AND SUN: water is the master currency; daytime heat drains hydration fast outside shade, nights are cold and hunted. Crucially, waked machines ALSO drink — every automation layer adds mouths, so your own success keeps thirst pressure alive into hour 100. (2) THE SIFTING: directional sandstorms on a worsening seasonal clock (the god's decay accelerating) scour the map — burying unmaintained structures, eroding exposed machinery, closing passes and exhuming new ruins. Storms are telegraphed 1-2 in-game days ahead by obelisk song and have a direction, so they are a planning problem (seal, mothball-bury, evacuate, or chase the storm to loot what it exposes), not a chore. (3) REGARD: using relic machinery emits Hum; the dreaming god's immune system sends antibodies — weeping Mourner colossi, glass wasps, salt angels — proportional to your total Hum, targeted at your loudest districts. This is the Factorio pollution loop reframed so it can never be turned off: the endgame (reassembling organs) requires ever more Hum. Pressure stays interesting because each source demands a different verb (migrate, bury, tune down, decoy, fight) and they interact — e.g. a storm that buries your obelisks silences your Hum, giving you a stealth window.

### Goal 2 — the automation layers

Tier-0 manual life: hauling water in jars, digging sand by hand, sweeping drift off structures, chipping copper and salt, pressing marrow-oil, firing clay, standing night watch. Automation = reawakening relic-machinery, and every machine is livestock, not infrastructure: it must be fed libations, it levels up with continuous work, and neglected machines go feral or fall back asleep. Layers: (1) WAKED GOLEMS follow cartouche paths — glyph routes you literally draw in the sand — hauling and digging; this is the belt-analog, but routes are cheap, redrawable, and storm-erasable. (2) SINGING OBELISKS project work-songs over line-of-sight songlines: combined power grid and command network; kills per-golem micromanagement and doubles as storm early-warning. (3) CLOCKWORK SHRINES automate crafting when fed offerings — the Still-Shrine condenses night dew into water, the Kiln-Womb fires golem parts and ceramic armor, the Loom of Names inscribes glyphs. (4) CISTERN-HEARTS AND AQUEDUCT VEINS — the god's repurposed arteries — pipe water/oil/ichor underground, ending liquid hauling and surviving burial. (5) THE WAKING CHOIR: harmonized obelisk arrays that herd dunes, deflect storm fronts, and sing defensive hymns that repel antibodies. (6) GOD-LIMB ENGINES: reassembled organs — a Heart that pumps an aquifer, an Eye that reveals buried strata server-wide, a Hand-titan that terraforms. Freshness vs belts: logistics is drawn routes plus buried arteries; power is geometric song, not wires; machines eat, sleep, level, and can be deliberately buried to mothball them through storms — your factory is a herd you shepherd, not a diagram you lay.

### Goal 2 — the sacrifice economy

Three currencies of sacrifice, each cutting a different nerve. (1) LIBATION: waking any machine costs a one-time dowry plus a daily tithe of water and oil — the exact resources your body needs. A golem is a second mouth at the well. (2) NAMES: large machines must be bound with a glyph-name, and names are scarce exploration finds; a bound name is locked until you dismantle the machine, destroying its accumulated levels. Names are the server's strategic reserve and the main thing 20 players argue about. (3) VIGOR: any wake can be paid in your own body — permanently reduced hydration cap — the desperate soloist's option. Worked minute-30 decision: you hold 40 water; a Well-Golem costs a 25-water dowry, drinks 3/day, but hauls 30/day from the far oasis — and the obelisks say a storm lands in 2 days, which you need 10 sealed-in water to survive. Wake it now on razor margins, or lose two days of hauling? Worked hour-20 decision: you finally pull the rare name AHM from a tomb. Bind it to a Choir Conductor to automate storm-deflection over the main base (ends sweeping labor forever, but +40 percent Hum means heavier raids), spend it on a Hand-titan to excavate the Reliquary quarter the last storm exposed before the next one reburies it (a one-storm window), or honor your co-op partner's claim on it for a second Still-Shrine at the forward camp. The hurt is good because every sacrifice is legible in the same units you drink and sweat in.

### Goal 4 — the map changes the plan

Each seed varies the fall-pattern of the god (which organs landed where, and shattered how), aquifer depth, prevailing storm corridors, migrating-oasis circuits, and storm-shadowing terrain like basalt reefs and glass fields. Examples: (1) dealt a shallow aquifer directly under a storm corridor, my strategy became a buried arcology — cistern base underground, arteries instead of surface routes, everything mothballed under sand at storm-time with only obelisk masts above; (2) dealt no permanent water, only a circuit of migrating oases (the god's wandering tears), we went full nomad — shrine-sledges, a golem caravan, cache stations at each oasis stop, and storms became a tailwind because we looted whatever they freshly exposed; (3) dealt the god's Heart just 2 km out but inside a high-Regard glass field, we rushed it — accepted brutal early raids for infinite pumped water by hour 8, a completely different opening than either of the above.

### Goal 5 — base ⇄ combat loop

Base to combat: (1) the Kiln-Womb fires ceramic plate armor and glass-edged khopeshes whose quality scales with sustained kiln heat — i.e., with your fuel automation; (2) leaving base inside songline range charges you with resonance — a shield/haste buff that decays with distance from your obelisks, so extending songlines toward a battlefield is literal power projection; (3) fed war-golems march with you as a squad — squad size is your oil economy made visible. Combat to base: (1) Mourners drop grief-salt, the only catalyst for the Still-Shrine's high-yield mode — your water economy wants you hunting; (2) blueprints are HYMNS, and you learn them from the god's own creatures and ruins — harvest a Salt Angel's larynx and your Cantor can teach the Choir a new defensive hymn; (3) feral golems guard relic sites, and defeating one without shattering its core lets you re-bind it — automation captured through combat, arriving home already leveled.

### Goals 3 + 6 — many paths, no milestones

There is no research tree. Capability comes from three orthogonal, order-free systems: HYMNS (blueprints learned from wherever you happen to dig and hunt — different seeds and different play yields different unlock orders), NAMES (a fungible, scarce bind-budget that gates scale rather than kind), and MACHINE GENERATIONS (a golem that hauls for ten hours becomes a Porter-Elder that can teach its level to fresh golems — investment paths diverge per server). Macro-progression is a continuous analog — percent of the god reassembled — with every organ optional and any order valid. Discovered archetypes: THE ROOT, an entrenched high-Hum arcology around one mega-cistern turtled behind a Waking Choir; THE PROCESSION, a low-Hum nomad caravan that never sleeps twice in one place and grows by capturing feral machines; THE PARLIAMENT OF SHRINES, a decentralized web of small automated outposts linked by songlines, spreading Hum so thin that raids stay small everywhere; and the ASCETIC line, near-zero automation, paying in vigor and raiding tombs for consumables — viable co-op specialists on any server.

### Goal 7 — beauty pays rent

CONSONANCE. Every waked machine contributes a voice — pitch and timbre by type — to the songlines that power it, and the geometry of your build IS the acoustics: symmetric placements, machine spacings at integer ratios of a songline's wavelength, enclosed courtyards around obelisks, and clean radial sightlines produce consonance. Consonant districts run faster, drink less, and soothe Regard; dissonant sprawl is loud — it attracts antibodies and erodes machine morale toward feralness. Separately, shade is functional architecture: colonnades and aligned awnings cast shade corridors that are your hydration-safe highways. This is not a bolted-on beauty meter because the optimization target is literally classical composition — rhythm, symmetry, negative space (machines need breathing room) — and the payoff is diegetic: the game plays your base back to you as music, and you can hear a refactor improve it before you read a single tooltip.

### Goal 8 — why you're doing this

Outer frame: you are the last congregation of the fallen god; the desert is its decaying corpse and the decay is accelerating — the worsening storm-seasons make the deadline felt, not told. Outer goal: reassemble enough of the god to decide what it becomes — wake it, grant it rest, or seat your own names in the Reliquary and replace it — a standing server-level choice, not a quest chain. Four cascading mechanisms keep 'why am I doing this' answered at every zoom level: (1) the Chronicle of Thirst — the world is drying because the god died, so your moment-to-moment water loop IS the stakes; (2) hymns-as-memory — every blueprint learned plays a fragment of the god's memory, so acquiring capability is literally learning the story, with nothing gated behind cutscenes; (3) Regard — the antagonist is the god reacting to your progress, so escalation is authored by your own choices; (4) organ-waking visibly rewrites world rules (wake the Heart and dead rivers briefly rerun; wake the Eye and a second moon reveals buried strata), so the next concrete objective is always self-evident: reach the next organ, feed the ones you've waked, and argue with your server about the ending.

## Multiplayer (2–20, persistent)

2-20 players on a persistent world with roles the fiction produces naturally: Cantors (obelisk layout and tuning), Quarriers (tombs, names, hymn-hunting combat), Waterwrights (artery and cistern logistics), Shepherds (golem feeding, leveling, caravans). Offline handling is elegant in-fiction: machines keep working while fed from communal tithe cisterns, and any player can set their district to auto-bury on logout — buried machines sleep, safe from storms and raids but earning nothing, so offline is protection-without-progress rather than grief or free income. Pressure scales with population: Regard keys off total server Hum plus per-player 'warmth', so raids grow with concurrency, and per-seed oasis output is fixed, so 20 players face genuine scarcity that pushes them into district crews or splinter nomad bands. The ending is a ritual requiring multiple players' names seated in the Reliquary — endgame is server politics by design.

## Closest existing games

- Factorio — machines are thirsty livestock, not belts; pollution becomes divine attention; no research tree
- Don't Starve Together — similar relentless co-op pressure, but with a deep automation endgame
- Against the Storm — storm-cycle pressure, but persistent real-time co-op, not roguelite runs
- Timberborn — water as master currency, plus combat and a self-reburying living map
- Core Keeper — comparable browser-scale top-down co-op, plus sacrifice economy and acoustic building

## Designer-admitted risks

- Water as both fuel and survival stat can death-spiral a whole server (storm plus thirsty machines equals unrecoverable state); machines falling asleep instead of dying softens it, but tuning that knife-edge across 2 vs 20 concurrent players is genuinely hard.
- Sand reburial attacks the core automation fantasy — players hate losing factories. If mothball-burial doesn't read as strategy, we either soften storms until goal 1 dies or keep them and churn players; this needs early, brutal playtesting.
- Consonance scoring risks collapsing into one solved meta-geometry (every server builds the same radial plaza), turning the beauty mechanic into a snowflake-grid; wavelength variation per obelisk type helps but is unproven.
- Exploration-driven unlocks (hymns) without a tech tree can hard-stall a bad seed — e.g. no Still-Shrine hymn found means water hell — and any pity system risks quietly becoming the linear quest line goal 6 forbids; late-joiner catch-up on persistent servers compounds this.
- Browser tech: persistent sand deformation, fluid arteries, and real-time audio synthesis of songlines for 20 players is a heavy stack for a small team; if the audible-base feature gets cut for performance, the aesthetics mechanic loses its payoff and goal 7 is gutted.

**Biggest weakness.** Water as simultaneous survival stat, machine fuel, and master currency puts the entire game on a knife-edge between two failure modes the pitch itself documents: unrecoverable server-wide death spirals (its own risk #1) and pressure trivialization (its own map example boasts "infinite pumped water by hour 8"). Every headline pressure has an authored automation counter — Choirs deflect storms, hymns repel antibodies, the Heart deletes thirst — so goal 1's "constant pressure" survives only if tuning threads a needle across 2-to-20-player concurrency that the team admits is genuinely hard. The pitch's freshest ideas are all downstream of the mechanic most likely to break.

**How a real server dies (failure scenario).** Hour 14, a three-player weeknight server. They waked a Well-Golem, a Kiln-Womb, and two haulers on razor margins because the game's minute-30 math encouraged exactly that; the obelisks telegraph a storm and they seal in with 11 water — but the tithe still drains 5 a day and the storm's tail tracks along the corridor the seed put over their base. Machines miss their libations mid-storm and fall asleep; when the sand clears, the golems are buried, the cistern is dry, and the nearest oasis has migrated behind a closed pass. One player paid Vigor twice, so his reduced hydration cap can't even cover the round trip. The only path back is two real-time hours of jar-hauling and hand-digging — the exact tier-0 grind they sacrificed to escape, now with zero meaningful choice because there is exactly one thing to do. They log off; on a persistent server the unfed base finishes starving overnight, and the next login shows a feral, buried ruin — a full restart with no death screen. Nobody returns, and the "protection without progress" logout feature never triggered because nobody knew the session was ending.

**The steelman fix.** Adopt one design law: pressure is transformed, never deleted — the Heart pumps only while fed an escalating tithe and radiates Hum, Choir deflection reroutes storms onto territory you still care about, repelling hymns raise baseline Regard. Pair it with a recovery floor: asleep machines re-wake at a steep discount, so a busted server bottoms out at slow-manual-rebuild instead of a logout spiral. This single rule rescues goal 1's late game, closes the "infinite water by hour 8" hole, and defuses the pitch's own #1 risk without softening the desert.

**Distinctiveness (4/5).** Closest game: Factorio. The skeleton is Factorio in a burial shroud — Hum is pollution, antibodies are biters, hymns are the tech tree relocated into the world — but no shipped game combines livestock-machines, acoustic base scoring, and a self-reburying map, so the difference matters if and only if those three survive production cuts.

**Feasibility (2/5).** Persistent, network-synced sand deformation is the budget-killer: it continuously invalidates golem pathfinding, burial state, songline line-of-sight, and drawn cartouche routes for up to 20 clients on a persistent server, so every other system pays a permanent tax to it. WebAudio makes the choir cheaper than the pitch fears, but a consonance scorer that is both computable and actually rewards beauty rather than one solved plaza is an unbudgeted research problem. The cut list under schedule pressure hits exactly the features (reburial, audio) that goals 1 and 7 depend on — the design has no cheap fallback shape.
