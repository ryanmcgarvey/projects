# 8. EMBERDEEP

> Seed: `abyss` · Goal fit **32/40** · Distinctiveness **4/5** · Feasibility **2/5** · Verdict: **promising**

**Pitch.** A real-time co-op survival-automation game on the floor of a lightless ocean, where your colony taps dying hydrothermal vents for every watt, breath, and degree of warmth. Every machine you run glows, hums, and heats the water — and the abyss can see, hear, and feel all of it. Industrialize fast enough to outrun the cooling of your world, or garden the dark into an ally.

**The hook.** A Factorio-like where every watt you burn is visible, audible bait, your power plants are dying geology you decide how fast to bleed, and your most beautiful garden is your best defense.

**Tone.** Cozy-dread: a pocket of amber warmth held against an indifferent black immensity; every lamp feels earned, every alarm feels personal. Art direction is light-on-black top-down 2D (WebGL bloom, sonar arcs, bioluminescent palettes, heat-shimmer shaders over silhouetted terrain) — darkness keeps the asset budget small for a browser team while making the visuals the fantasy.

## How it serves the founding goals

### Goal 1 — constant pressure

Three coupled clocks that never stop. (1) THE METABOLIC DRAW: every player, habitat, and tamed creature burns oxygen and heat per tick. O2 comes from powered electrolyzers and cathode gardens; power comes from vent heat gradients; so a power dip cascades into an air countdown within minutes. Early game this is a wrist gauge; late game it is a colony-wide Ledger, but the chain never breaks. (2) VENT SENESCENCE: every vent has a decline curve, and drawing harder physically scales its throat with mineral deposits, hastening its death — your energy base is not a resource patch, it is a failing organ you are bleeding, and you choose the bleed rate. Late game you never "solve" power; you manage a portfolio of dying vents across a ridge. (3) THE SIGNATURE: your colony's heat plume, light bloom, and noise are summed into a sensory footprint that sets ecological attention — grazers come for your bacteria mats, marrow eels chew warm pipes to drink the fluid, hatchet swarms mob lights, leviathans key on sustained noise. Growth directly manufactures threat, but each channel has distinct counterplay (dump waste heat into dense brine pools, hood your lumen strains, baffle your pumps, build decoy beacons). Pressure stays interesting rather than chore-like because it is answered by architecture and routing decisions, not upkeep clicks — automation absorbs the chores while the pressure moves up an abstraction level — and because the sources drift: vents fail, fauna habituate to overused decoys, and layouts that were safe at hour 5 are loud and obsolete at hour 25.

### Goal 2 — the automation layers

Tier-0 manual life: scraping chemosynthetic bacteria mats with a hand tool, pumping bloomery bellows, ferrying ore in your personal sub, cracking chlorate candles for emergency air, holding a lure-lamp to net fish. Automation layers: (1) HEATWORKS — thermocouple stacks straddling vent mouths plus heat piping; replaces hand-fed heat and hand-cranked power, and makes layout thermodynamic (your own waste heat near a stack ruins its gradient). (2) CHEMOSTAT VATS — continuous bacteria culture fed by vent-fluid pumps; replaces mat-scraping, yields biomass, sulfur, and feedstock. (3) SLURRY HYDRAULICS — grinders, pumps, and brine-density sorting columns that settle mixed slurry into strata; replaces hand-sorting and short-haul carrying, and is gorgeous (glowing sediment columns instead of splitters). (4) DROPLINE AND BLADDER LOGISTICS — ballast-weighted cargo descends cables, hydrogen bladders (electrolysis byproduct) lift goods up depth terraces; replaces sub milk-runs; the H2 is the same H2 your fuel cells want. (5) PHEROMONE LABOR — tamed porter crabs and drift rays follow laid scent trails to haul, graze pests, and patrol; replaces road-network buildout over rough terrain but must be fed and kept calm. (6) REMORA DRONES — autonomous mini-subs running survey and harvest sorties along a sonar beacon net, which itself emits noise and raises your Signature. Why fresh vs belts: logistics is buoyancy, fluid density, and animal behavior instead of belts and inserters; power is a gradient your own architecture can spoil; and half the "machines" are alive — an automation stack you must keep fed, lit, and unafraid.

### Goal 2 — the sacrifice economy

The core UI is THE MANIFOLD: valves splitting scarce flows between survival and industry. Electrolyzer O2 splits three ways — lungs / smelter oxidation / bioreactor aeration — so industry literally breathes your air. Vent heat is a finite kW budget split between habitat warmth, thermoelectric power, and process heat; overdrawing scales the vent throat, spending its total lifespan for present output (a mortgage on your power plant). Quiet/dark equivalents of every machine cost 2-3x (baffled pumps, hooded lumen strains), so safety is bought only at growth's expense; expansion to a new tap stretches the calm-field perimeter your gardens must cover. Manual grinding always remains the zero-capital fallback, so the choice is never blocked, only priced in your time. MINUTE-30 WORKED EXAMPLE: your electrolyzer makes 10 O2/min; your suit holds 300 and reads 140. The bloomery needs a 60-O2 burn to smelt your hand-mined 40 chalcopyrite into copper for a second thermocouple stack (+80% power, compounding). Sonar shows a lithium-rich brine pool 600m out; your starter vent just pulsed down 20% output; a marrow eel has been circling the warm outflow pipe. Options: smelt now (power compounds, but no scouting for 15 minutes and the eel stays interested), bank O2 and scout (map knowledge plus lithium toward piezo fencing), or spend your only 12 copper on a piezo fence immediately (safety, delaying the power curve). All three are defensible; all three visibly cost something the other two wanted.

### Goal 4 — the map changes the plan

Procedural variation: vent-line topology and temperament (many steady weak "smokers" vs a few violent pulsing "screamers"), brine pool placement (dense toxic lakes — impassable, lithium-rich, and the best heat dump), cold methane seeps (an entire alternate combustion-fuel chemistry), current channels (free bulk drift logistics and turbine power, but permanently noisy), depth-terrace layout (pressure-gates the geography by hull rating), leviathan home ranges, and whale falls (timed organic windfalls that summon scavenger storms). Worked reactions: (a) dealt a ridge of many weak smokers, your strategy becomes distributed micro-bases linked by droplines and pheromone trails, low Signature, shepherd play; (b) dealt one giant screamer, you build a compact fortress economy — big smelters, heavy Signature, False Beacons and turrets, corsair escorts for every convoy; (c) dealt a methane seep basin near spawn but with the only current channel running through a leviathan's range, you retool to combustion heat (which must be dumped into a brine pool to stay quiet on the thermal channel) and route logistics the slow overland way — effectively paying rent to a monster you may eventually evict.

### Goal 5 — base ⇄ combat loop

Combat is real-time sub piloting (twin-stick feel: sonar pings, torpedoes, arc lances, ink and decoy pods) plus base defense architecture. Base to combat: (1) a foundry alloy line yields Hull-II/III sub plating, which is the only key to deeper pressure terraces and leviathan basins; (2) an H2 fuel-cell chain extends sortie range and charges arc-lance capacitors at base substations — your weapon uptime is literally your power grid; (3) gardens culture ink-polyp countermeasure pods and lumen-camo skin for stealth approaches, and torpedo warheads run on chemostat sulfur. Combat to base: (1) the Anglermaw (a leviathan whose lure mimics your own beacons) drops its lure organ, buildable as a False Beacon tower that redirects swarm aggro; (2) Dreadnought Siphonophore gas bladders become large buoyancy platforms, enabling mobile flotilla base modules; (3) marrow eel thermoglands become counterflow heat exchangers that roughly double heat-pipe efficiency. Victories reshape rather than erase threat: deterring or killing a basin's apex predator opens its vent cluster and brine pool to industry, but releases the mid-web — grazer populations bloom and your mats need new fencing.

### Goals 3 + 6 — many paths, no milestones

There is no research tree. Capability is three continuous envelopes plus a crafting grammar. Envelopes: heat budget (kW actually secured), pressure envelope (depth rating of hulls/structures from materials you can really produce), and ecological rapport (species studied, tamed, cultured). Grammar: machines assemble from modules — heat source x chamber x medium x catalyst — so "recipes" are discovered combinations, hinted by dissecting fauna and salvaging predecessor wrecks rather than unlocked from a list; which knowledge is even reachable depends on which species and geology your map rolled and which basins you chose to open. Progression is therefore a position in a continuous space, not a checklist. Genuinely different archetypes observed in design tests on paper: THERMAL BARON (screamer-vent fortress, alloy and torpedo economy, loud, armored, decoy-heavy); SYMBIONT SHEPHERD (low-Signature garden colony, guardian fauna, pheromone logistics, near-pacifist, wins by being unappetizing); NOMAD FLOTILLA (buoyancy-platform mobile base chasing vent flare-ups and whale falls, sub-centric, owns nothing it cannot lift); BRINE CHEMIST (cold-seep methane and lithium chemistry, nearly vent-independent, trades heat problems for toxicity handling).

### Goal 7 — beauty pays rent

The garden IS the meter — there is no beauty score, only a small, real ecological simulation whose outputs the colony depends on: light, aggro-calming pheromone fields, filtration, and O2 from cathode gardens. Lumen polyps brighten when lantern shrimp visit them; shrimp need chemostat overflow within range plus shadowed rest niches; three or more compatible species interleaved trigger mutualism multipliers; monoculture rows attract blight grazers; nutrients ride the water current, so productive gardens curve along flow lines and alternate light with deliberate shadow. The sim's optima were chosen to coincide with what eyes like: the best garden is diverse, curved, interlaced, glowing in gradients — reef-beautiful by construction, not by decree. And it is load-bearing: gardens are your perimeter lighting, your calm-field that raises predator approach thresholds, and your offline resilience (a diverse garden self-sustains during server torpor; a monoculture rots). Heat architecture adds a second aesthetic layer: convection is rendered as visible shimmer, and thermally efficient layouts naturally read as organic chimneyed forms rather than grids.

### Goal 8 — why you're doing this

Outer frame: the ridge is entering the Long Cooling. The vent field your ancestors' wreck-colony drinks from is measurably dying, and the colony must secure a future that outlives it — a horizon, not a quest chain. Four mechanisms cascade from that horizon to the current minute: (1) THE LEDGER — always-visible colony margins (heat, air, Signature) tie every act to "keep the flame lit"; (2) THE VITALITY FORECAST — seismic surveys date each vent's decline, so mid-term goals generate themselves from the map rather than from a script; (3) THE THREE FUTURES — colony-scale gradients advanced by ordinary play, never selected from a menu: the Deep Tap (drill to the magma chamber), the Closed Garden (ecology self-sufficient without vents), the Great Migration (reach the rumored Mother Smoker up-ridge); your playstyle drifts you toward one, and drift is reversible; (4) BEACONS OF THE FALLEN — predecessor colonies' wrecks carry logs, module hints, and cautionary tableaux (a chimney choked to death by overdraw), teaching mechanics and stakes in whatever order the map allows. HOUR-20 WORKED EXAMPLE: the Forecast shows your Twin Screamers entering decline in about 4 hours of runtime. A scouted virgin field 2.4 km north sits inside a Dreadnought Siphonophore's range (evicting it needs three Hull-III subs, ~800 alloy, and retooling your dropline capacity to torpedo assembly for two hours). A leviathan-free methane basin lies east, but switching to combustion triples your thermal Signature and means a base-wide refit while margins shrink. Or you commit to the Deep Tap at home — a drill whose draw will brown-out your defenses for the duration. Four players, three futures, one Ledger; the argument at the table is the game.

## Multiplayer (2–20, persistent)

Roles fall out of the pressure channels, not a class system: Ventwright (heat and power architecture), Gardener (ecology, light, calm-field, offline resilience), Corsair (subs, hunts, escorts, whale-fall races), Prospector (sonar cartography, seismic surveys, wreck dives). Two players wear two hats each; twenty players naturally form daughter colonies along the ridge, linked by droplines, each with a local Ledger feeding the shared one — schism is supported, not prevented, and a Migration-faction splitting off from a Deep-Tap-faction is an intended story. Offline: when the last player logs out the colony auto-torpors — valves close to survival minimum, reserves drain slowly, no leviathan raids occur, but grazers nibble unfenced assets; how much you wake up to is determined by deliberate pre-logout investments (banked brine-batteries, garden diversity, fencing), making offline safety itself part of the sacrifice economy. Pressure scales with population: Signature grows with per-capita industry, the leviathan attention budget scales with active player count, and vent draw-down accelerates, pushing larger servers up the ridge sooner.

## Closest existing games

- Factorio — pollution becomes three sensed channels; belts become buoyancy, fluids, and animals
- Subnautica — its dread and awe, but persistent co-op colony automation instead of solo story
- Oxygen Not Included — coupled life-support budgets, but real-time, combat, multiplayer, one shared map
- Barotrauma — co-op submarine terror, plus a persistent industrial home worth defending
- Core Keeper — top-down co-op mining base at browser scale, but systemic ecology instead of boss-gated progression

## Designer-admitted risks

- Server simulation cost: heat diffusion + ecology agents + fluid logistics for up to 20 players on commodity hosting (fly.io-class) is a real engineering risk; requires coarse-cell aggregation and aggressive tick LOD from day one or the design is unshippable.
- Aggro legibility: three emission channels (heat/light/noise) can read as random attacks; the design demands exceptional sensory UI — Signature overlays and post-attack 'why it came' forensics — or the flagship pressure system becomes frustration.
- Grammar crafting discoverability: module-combination recipes without a tree risk wiki-dependence and can collapse into an implicit tree anyway; needs strong in-fiction hinting (dissection, wreck salvage) and generous near-miss feedback, and balancing an open grammar is far harder than balancing a list.
- Forced migration vs base attachment: vent senescence threatens the base-builder audience's core pleasure (a home you keep); if decline pacing is too aggressive or flotilla tooling is not genuinely fun, players will experience the central mechanic as the game deleting their work.
- Darkness vs factory readability: light-as-art fights at-a-glance factory legibility; the likely fix — an ugly-clear sonar/schematic view alongside the beautiful-dark view — risks players living permanently in the ugly view, silently killing the aesthetic that sells the game.

**Biggest weakness.** The setting's signature mechanic — dying vents forcing portfolio management and eventual migration — is structurally at war with the two pleasures the loop goals depend on: automation that pays off (goal 2) and a home worth defending (goal 5). In Factorio the psychological payoff of investment is permanence while you climb the abstraction ladder; here every heatworks stands on geology scheduled to die, so the mid-game defaults to a treadmill of rebuilding rather than compounding. The pitch's own risk list admits players may "experience the central mechanic as the game deleting their work" — that is not an edge case, it is the design's default trajectory as written.

**How a real server dies (failure scenario).** Hour 14, a Tuesday-night trio on their third session. The Forecast says their main vent dies in three hours of runtime; ten hours of heatworks, slurry columns, and a garden tuned to this exact flow line cannot move. Migration means rebuilding everything 2.4 km away under a siphonophore while all three clocks keep running, and the "fun" flotilla tooling is locked behind buoyancy-platform recipes the crafting grammar has never hinted at — so one player opens a wiki tab, finds the one known-good garden lattice and the one baffled-pump meta, and the open grammar collapses into a worse tech tree. The group grinds a joyless two-session relocation entirely in the sonar schematic view because the beautiful dark is unreadable at factory scale, the new vent pulses down 20% mid-refit, the O2-power cascade wipes the half-built colony in four minutes, and nobody books a fourth session. Every flagship system — senescence, grammar, darkness, coupled clocks — contributed to the quit.

**The steelman fix.** Decouple senescence from erasure: when a vent dies, the infrastructure on it stays valuable — pipes, gardens, droplines, and structures remain fully usable when re-fed by heat piped in from newly tapped vents, so the colony compounds along the ridge instead of relocating. Senescence then generates expansion pressure ("tap or die") while preserving the permanence that makes automation investment and base attachment actually pay off, and cold districts awaiting re-heating are a better story beat than an abandoned base. This one change resolves the pitch's worst internal contradiction without touching any other system.

**Distinctiveness (4/5).** Closest game: Oxygen Not Included. ONI already owns coupled life-support-as-factory and Factorio already owns pollution-summons-enemies, so this is a recombination rather than virgin territory — but real-time co-op persistence plus attention-as-three-channel-pollution is a genuine gap among shipped games, and the difference matters if (and only if) the aggro system stays legible.

**Feasibility (2/5).** The design demands two architectures that fight each other: a heavy server-authoritative coupled simulation (heat diffusion, fluid logistics, ecology agents with habituation and pheromone trails, persistent offline torpor) and low-latency client-predicted twin-stick combat netcode, both in-browser on fly.io-class hosting for up to 20 players. Add dual-view rendering (dark-beautiful plus sonar-schematic) and procedural vent/current/terrace generation and this is multi-year scope for a small team; the pitch's 'coarse-cell aggregation from day one' is a hand-wave, and coarsening the sim is exactly where the legibility promises ('why it came' forensics) will quietly die.
