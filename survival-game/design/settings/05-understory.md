# 5. UNDERSTORY (working title) — A Tithe to the Old Growth

> Seed: `mycel` · Goal fit **33/40** · Distinctiveness **4/5** · Feasibility **2/5** · Verdict: **promising**

**Pitch.** A browser-based co-op survival-automation game where your "factory" is a living garden of symbiotic organisms grown inside a vast, sentient old-growth forest that is forever growing back over everything you make. Every layer of automation is bought by redirecting the sap, land, and safety your survival depends on, and over-extraction is answered not by a pollution meter but by the forest itself — sprites that steal, blooms that strangle, megafauna that migrate through your trunk lines. The twist: ecologically harmonious, beautiful bases literally run better and get reclaimed last, because the forest keeps what it loves.

**The hook.** A Factorio-like where the factory is a living garden a sentient forest is forever reclaiming — and the most beautiful garden is also the most productive and best-defended one, because the forest keeps what it loves.

**Tone.** Painterly top-down 2D (hand-painted tile flora, parallax canopy shadow, cheap shader wind-sway and drifting spore motes — very shippable by a small team to browsers), palette moving from mossy Mononoke greens by day to bioluminescent blues at night to blood-red wrath blooms. Emotionally: tender and menacing at once — Ghibli warmth with real teeth; you love the thing that is trying to eat your base.

## How it serves the founding goals

### Goal 1 — constant pressure

Three interlocking pressures, all generated by the player's own success rather than a timer. (1) RECLAMATION: the forest constantly regrows over structures, paths, and clearings; untended organisms go feral — a starved snap-lily turret starts shooting at you, an unfed porter beetle raids your gourds. Scales with base surface area: the bigger your works, the longer your frontier of upkeep. (2) GRAFT HUNGER: you are a Wardened — a dying person the forest saved with a heart-graft — and the graft demands feeding on an escalating refinement ladder (raw sap → amber sap → heartsap → chorded heartsap). Every combat graft you install ratchets appetite up, so the food problem re-tiers forever instead of being solved once. (3) IRE: a per-watershed ledger of your extraction RATE; the forest responds proportionally and legibly, from thieving sprites at low Ire to Wrath Blooms, thornback elk migrations, and an angered Elder at high Ire. Late game adds the Grey Hush, a dead-silence front creeping in from the map rim that hushed land spawns hollowed beasts from — so even a fully pacified watershed is never an endpoint. Why it stays interesting rather than chore-like: pressure is directional and telegraphed (the forest probes your least-harmonious district; wind and bloom forecasts are readable), the counter-play IS the core game (the same growth force strangling you is what you channel into automation), and mature, harmonious districts genuinely self-defend — so pressure migrates to wherever you are currently expanding, which is exactly where your attention already is.

### Goal 2 — the automation layers

Metaphor: gardening and symbiosis. You never place machines — you seed, graft, feed, and train organisms, then wean them toward self-sufficiency. Tier-0 manual tasks: hand-foraging mushrooms/berries, knife-tapping resin, hauling in a back-basket, hand-pollinating cultivars, hand-watering, splicing at the graft bench, standing night watch. Automation layers: (1) PORTER SYMBIONTS — loam-hog beetles, later marsh-striders, that haul along pheromone trails you paint; alleviates hauling; they must be fed and can be predated, so routes are living things. (2) ROOT DUCTS — grafted ductlings grow into sap pipelines between nodes, but ducts grow along moisture and refuse stone, so routing is a negotiation with terrain, not free placement. (3) CULTIVAR GROVES — self-fruiting milkcap rings, spigot-grafted resin pines, hopper-gourds; alleviates foraging; yield depends on surrounding companion ecology, not just placement. (4) SPOREWORKS — puffball signal relays plus twitch-vine actuators form the logic layer: "when hopper-gourd full, fire spore, close sphincter valve." Signals are literal drifting spore clouds with wind-dependent latency. (5) WARDEN SYMBIONTS — thornhound kennels, snap-lily batteries, lantern-wisp patrols; alleviates night watch and skirmish defense. (6) THE CHORUS — endgame: tithe entire groves back to the mycelial mind and it moves goods for you through its own underground network, logistics bought with territory. Freshness vs Factorio belts: throughput is metabolic, not fixed — a function of health, feeding, season, and neighboring ecology; automation can starve, over-fruit, or go feral; and your logistics network shares a biology with the threat, so the line between "my factory" and "the hostile world" is porous by design.

### Goal 2 — the sacrifice economy

The survival loop and the growth loop draw from one pool. Sap is simultaneously food, fuel, and growth medium; Marrowmeal compost is brewed from edible ingredients; and every large graft must be SEEDED WITH HEARTSAP drained directly from your character's graft reserve — you literally spend your health buffer to grow infrastructure. Territory is sacrificed via tithes: Chorus logistics and Elder pacts require ceding developed land under permanent no-extraction covenant. Safety is sacrificed via Ire: rushing automation through heavy extraction invites escalating pushback. And time is always the fallback: everything can be done manually, so "grind instead of invest" is a standing option. MINUTE-30 WORKED EXAMPLE: you hold 4 amber sap with graft-hunger ticking and sporefall (hostile night) approaching. (a) Drink them — safe through the night; (b) feed 3 to a ductling to grow your first pipeline from the resin pines to camp — pays off in ~10 minutes, but you face sporefall hungry and must night-forage; (c) use 2 to bait-tame a porter grub you spotted — hauling solved, but it eats daily. Meanwhile thorn ivy will close your path to the pines in ~15 minutes unless you spend time cutting it back. HOUR-20 WORKED EXAMPLE: your watershed Ire crossed a threshold and the Grove Mother triggers a Wrath Bloom — in two in-game days a thornback elk migration will trample your duct trunk lines. (a) Militarize: grow a warden hedge + snap-lily battery, spending the 60 heartsap-lilies you had earmarked to seed a Chorus pact; (b) appease: tithe back your eastern milkcap grove (lose ~30% of food automation, Ire drops, herd reroutes); (c) engineer: pre-grow duct bypasses around the forecast path — huge sap cost, but the herd's passage leaves dung fields, a free fertilizer district if you hold on; (d) hunt the herd matriarch — high combat risk, spikes Ire further, but unlocks elk-sinew grafts nothing else provides. Each fork mortgages a different future, which is where the hurt lives.

### Goal 4 — the map changes the plan

The generator varies watershed/moisture gradients, soil types (loam, peat, stone), the layout of the forest's own mycelial trunk lines (which you can tap or ride), prevailing winds (which govern spore-signal reliability and pollen-storm exposure), species mix, and — most importantly — the temperament of each region's Elder spirit (a Grove Mother punishes extraction hard; a Rot Mother tolerates it but accelerates structural decay; a Drowned Choir floods but gifts fluid logistics). Concrete pivots: (1) "A stone ridge split my camp from the resin pines and ducts refuse stone, so I built a porter-beetle cliff-trail economy with a wind-gap spore relay — I ended up a creature-logistics specialist all game." (2) "I spawned in peat bog: terrible cultivar soil but rich in bog-iron and preserved ancient scions, so I rushed the reaver/excavation path and imported food via Chorus tithes instead of farming." (3) "My Elder was a Rot Mother, so heavy extraction was actually viable — but everything I built rotted 40% faster, pushing me toward regrowable living structures over dead-wood construction; in my previous world under a Grove Mother the same strategy would have summoned a Wrath Bloom by hour three."

### Goal 5 — base ⇄ combat loop

Base → combat: (1) a chitin-forge beetle nursery grows your armor plates, and plate quality scales with how well the nursery's ecology is fed — a better garden is literally better armor; (2) active combat grafts are supply-limited by base production: the Spore Lung (exhale a stun cloud) draws on puffball culture stocks, Bark-Skin tinctures on resin throughput — let the supply chain lapse and the ability dims mid-fight; (3) thornhounds raised in your kennels deploy as combat companions whose pack size and ferocity equal kennel quality at home. Combat/exploration → base: (1) defeat a Wrath-Boar and culture its gut flora to unlock the Iron-Belly Composter, which digests toxic bogwood into fuel; (2) fallen greater spirits drop grave-spores — cultivar seeds obtainable no other way, e.g. lantern-fungus lighting that passively soothes local Ire; (3) deep hollows and hushed ruins hold ancient graft-scions (blueprint-organisms) you carry home and propagate into new buildable lines. Bonus coupling: killing a regional Elder permanently shifts that region's weather and ecology, so combat outcomes rewrite the map your base economy depends on.

### Goals 3 + 6 — many paths, no milestones

There is no research tree. Capability = the Cultivar Codex, a living library of trait-bearing scions (sticky, luminous, hollow, hardy, signaling, carnivorous...) you discover, culture, and cross-breed; the trait-combination grammar is fixed and masterable, but WHICH species carry which traits varies per world and biome, so the route to "I have a pump" differs every playthrough and rewards experimentation over recipe memorization. Macro-structure is replaced by Elder chords: each regional Elder holds a chord needed at the Heartwood, earnable in any order and by any method — patient pact-building, extravagant gifts, or killing the Elder and prying the chord from its corpse (fast, but permanently scars that region's ecology). Genuinely different viable archetypes: (1) THE SYMBIOTE GARDEN — low-Ire, tithe-heavy, deep Chorus pacts; a small exquisite base where the forest itself does your logistics and combat is mostly declined via appeasement; (2) THE REAVER FORT — high-extraction thorn-walled fortress that farms wrath-beasts for scions, treats pushback as a renewable boss-spawner, and accepts feral-machine risk as an operating cost; (3) THE WATERSHED NOMAD — three or four small satellite camps riding the mycelial trunk lines, never concentrating Ire in any one watershed, logistics via porter caravans; owns little, angers no one, and is everywhere. Progression order is also free: defense before logistics, logistics before food automation, combat grafts before any base at all.

### Goal 7 — beauty pays rent

The Bloom system: every organism emits and requires auras — shade, nectar, moisture, birdsong, stillness — and wandering wisp-spirits dwell wherever aura webs are rich, buffing growth and passively absorbing Ire. Monoculture rows starve their own auras; interleaved plantings with curved hedgerows, ponds, canopy layering, and open glades maximize aura coverage and edge-length — so the throughput-optimal factory is, by the simulation's own math, a Ghibli garden. Beauty is also defense: reclamation targets the least-harmonious district first, because harmonious land reads to the forest as part of itself; an ugly monoculture block is the first thing the wild eats. This is not a bolted-on beauty meter — there is no beauty score anywhere in the code, just the one ecology simulation shared by production, pressure, and Ire, whose optimum happens to be diverse, curved, and lovely. The failure mode it avoids: since auras interlock like a placement puzzle (shade-lovers under nectar-emitters beside moisture wells), building well feels like solving, not decorating.

### Goal 8 — why you're doing this

Outer frame: you are a Wardened — the forest saved your life with a heart-graft, and you owe a bond-debt to a dying god. The Heartwood at the forest's center is failing; the Grey Hush, a dead silence, creeps inward from the map's rim, and when the Heartwood goes quiet, so does the graft keeping you alive. Outer goal: reach the Heartwood and rekindle it — or, on darker ledgers, claim it. Four cascade mechanisms keep "why am I doing this" always answered: (1) THE HUSH FRONT — visible, slow, dynamic; hushed land yields nothing and births hollowed beasts, giving a soft global clock and a permanent reason to grow capability, never a hard fail-state timer; (2) GRAFT HUNGER — the moment-to-moment survival loop IS the narrative in miniature: the heartsap failing in the Heartwood is the same substance you refine daily to stay alive, so improving your sap chain is literally researching the mystery; (3) ELDER CHORDS — the non-linear macro-goal: gather the chords of the forest's song from regional Elders in any order, by pact, gift, or force, each method changing world state; (4) THE TITHE LEDGER — everything you extract, tithe, kill, or restore is written into the forest's memory, and the options available at the Heartwood (rekindle, replace, usurp) are a continuous function of that ledger — no quest chain, no milestone gates, just a world keeping score of who you have been.

## Multiplayer (2–20, persistent)

Persistent small-server worlds (2-20) fit natively: division of labor falls out of the systems rather than classes — someone gravitates to grove-keeping (ecology/aura layouts), someone to beast-taming (porter routes, kennels, war-hounds), someone to reaving (scion hunts, Elder fights), someone to envoy work (pacts, gifts, Ire management). Ire is communal per-watershed, so one crew's reaving raises the pressure another crew's garden must absorb — deliberate, spicy co-op politics; at 12+ players the natural structure is multiple settlements in different watersheds under different Elders, trading scions and chords. Offline persistence: organisms keep living, so a well-balanced, aura-rich district keeps producing while you sleep — but reclamation and feral drift are throttled to near-zero when server population is zero, and feral machines idle rather than rampage unattended, so you return to overgrowth and hungry symbionts, never to rubble. Pressure scales with headcount automatically because Ire tracks extraction rate: more players extract more, so the forest pushes harder without any artificial difficulty dial; the Hush front's pace also scales gently with total server activity. Technically plausible: server-authoritative tick simulation (Factorio-style but far smaller entity counts), top-down 2D sprites, WebSocket sync — comfortable on a single fly.io node per world.

## Closest existing games

- Factorio — automation depth, but belts become creatures/roots and pollution fights back with intent
- Don't Starve Together — co-op survival tone-with-teeth, plus the deep automation layer it never had
- Against the Storm — hostile-forest economic pressure, but persistent real-time avatar play, no roguelite meta
- Terra Nil — ecological harmony as core mechanics, but with survival stakes, combat, and a persistent base
- Wildmender — garden-survival warmth, but with genuine logistics/automation intricacy and living pushback

## Designer-admitted risks

- Upkeep death-spiral: living machines that must be fed can compound into babysitting — upkeep-on-upkeep is exactly the chore goal 1 forbids; the design depends on mature automation reaching genuine self-sufficiency (closed feeding loops), which is a hard tuning problem with a narrow band between trivial and tyrannical.
- Mushiness vs puzzle clarity: guided organic growth (ducts that route themselves, aura-driven yields) risks losing the crisp, legible cause-and-effect that makes Factorio-likes satisfying; without excellent overlays and deterministic growth rules, players will experience the sim as vibes rather than a system they can master.
- The Bloom system is min-maxable into a new beauty meter: adjacency/aura systems are notoriously solvable, and the community will find one optimal 'pond-hedge tile' to stamp everywhere — collapsing goal 7 into decoration-by-spreadsheet; per-world species/trait variance and Elder temperament help but are unproven countermeasures.
- Browser/server performance: thousands of metabolizing, growing, pathing organisms simulated server-side for a 20-player persistent world on commodity hosting is a real engineering wall; the obvious fix (aggressive region sleeping) directly conflicts with the promise that the world keeps living while you're away.
- Genre-fantasy friction and social friction: a game that punishes extraction is arguing with the hoarding instinct automation players come for — mis-tuned Ire reads as the game hating its own genre; and communal per-watershed Ire is a built-in griefing vector (one reaver can drown a whole server's gardens in wrath) that needs governance mechanics the design doesn't yet have.

**Biggest weakness.** The load-bearing contradiction between goals 1 and 2: every automation layer is a mouth, so investment converts labor into upkeep rather than removing it, and the design's only answer — 'weaning toward self-sufficiency' — sits on a knife edge where success deletes the survival pressure (breaking goal 1) and failure produces a tamagotchi farm (breaking goal 2). The pitch lists this first among its own risks and offers a word, not a mechanism. Everything distinctive about the setting (living factory, porous threat/factory boundary) is downstream of this untuned hinge.

**How a real server dies (failure scenario).** Hour 14, a three-player server. The base has two porter-beetle routes, a duct trunk line, four cultivar groves, a kennel, and a snap-lily hedge — roughly twenty organisms with independent hunger clocks. Login now opens with a fixed 40-minute maintenance rotation: refill the kennel, re-paint pheromone trails washed out by rain, cut thorn ivy off the ducts, top up graft hunger, re-feed the hedge so it doesn't shoot at you — before any discretionary play begins. Player three, bored of gardening chores, went reaving in the next watershed and spiked the communal Ire ledger; a Wrath Bloom triggers while the group's sap reserve is fully committed to upkeep, and the elk migration tramples the trunk line, adding two more sessions of pure repair. The group runs the math and realizes base surface area is the enemy and the dominant strategy is to build almost nothing — the Watershed Nomad — at which point a base-building automation game has argued its players out of building or automating, and they quietly migrate back to Factorio, where a belt fed once is fed forever.

**The steelman fix.** Make weaning a concrete, legible, finite per-district puzzle: bind it to Bloom completion, where satisfying an explicit aura/companion checklist (coverage thresholds, one resident wisp) flips every organism in that district to guaranteed self-feeding and feral-proof, permanently. This fuses goals 1, 2, and 7 into a single mechanism — the beauty puzzle IS the thing that retires upkeep — converting babysitting from perpetual rent into a solvable end-state per district, while pressure provably migrates to the expansion frontier where the player's attention already is.

**Distinctiveness (4/5).** Closest game: Atrio: The Dark Wild. Atrio already shipped 'organic automation inside a hostile ecosystem,' but it is single-player and its ecology is a parts library rather than an antagonist with taste — the co-op persistence, communal Ire politics, and beauty-as-throughput layer differ enough to matter, provided the sim stays legible enough to read as a system rather than vibes.

**Feasibility (2/5).** The netcode and entity counts are honestly scoped — a coarse-tick server-authoritative sim on one fly.io node with 2-20 players is proven territory. The real costs are elsewhere: the organic art matrix (every species times growth, health, feral, and seasonal states, hand-painted) dwarfs modular machine sprites, and three interlocking pressure systems plus an ecology whose optimum must be simultaneously legible, balanced, and beautiful is a multi-year tuning problem the pitch itself calls a 'narrow band between trivial and tyrannical.' Budget the aura/Ire/moisture legibility overlays as a full engineering workstream, because without them risk #2 (players experience the sim as vibes) is the default outcome, not the edge case.
